# PMOS (formerly PCOS) Diet Guidelines

## What PMOS involves, nutritionally
PMOS is associated with insulin resistance in many cases, meaning the body's cells respond less efficiently to insulin, which can lead to higher blood sugar levels and, over time, weight gain that is often harder to manage than in people without this profile. Diet plays a supportive role in managing these effects, alongside medical care, though diet alone is not a treatment for the underlying condition.

## Core dietary principle: a balanced, sustainable eating pattern
There is no single diet that is best for everyone with PMOS. A sustainable, balanced eating pattern that emphasizes vegetables, whole grains, pulses, adequate protein, healthy fats and minimally processed foods can support metabolic health. Lower-GI, high-fibre carbohydrate choices may be useful for some people.

## Low-GI Indian food swaps
- Replace white rice with **brown rice, quinoa, or millets** (bajra, jowar, ragi)
- Choose **whole wheat or multigrain roti** over refined flour (maida) products
- Include **whole legumes** (rajma, chana, moong) which have a naturally low GI and high fiber
- Add **vegetables with every meal** to slow overall glucose absorption
- Choose fruits with lower GI (guava, apple, pear) over very ripe bananas or mango in large quantities

## Other supportive habits
- **Protein with every meal** (dal, paneer, eggs, curd) helps slow digestion and supports satiety
- **Healthy fats** (nuts, seeds, ghee in moderation) also slow glucose absorption
- Regular meal timing, rather than long gaps followed by large meals, supports more stable blood sugar
- Limiting sugary drinks and heavily processed snacks is particularly relevant for this profile

## On weight management
Weight loss, even a modest amount (around 5–10% of body weight in many cases), has been associated with meaningful improvement in insulin sensitivity and symptom management for some people with PMOS. The approach should focus on sustainable, whole-food eating patterns rather than restrictive or crash dieting, which can backfire and worsen the relationship with food and hormones.

## Important note
PMOS presents differently across individuals, and nutritional needs can vary based on other factors like thyroid function, weight, and symptom pattern. This document offers general, food-based guidance only. It does not replace personalized medical or dietetic advice, and anyone managing PMOS should work with a doctor or registered dietitian for a plan suited to their specific situation.
