# When to See a Doctor: Red Flag Symptoms

## Purpose of this document
Dietary and lifestyle guidance can support wellbeing, but it cannot replace medical diagnosis or treatment. This document exists to help identify situations where food-based advice is not enough, and a doctor should be consulted instead of, or alongside, continuing to focus on diet alone.

## General red flags (any nutrient concern)
- Persistent fatigue that does not improve with rest or diet changes over a couple of weeks
- Breathlessness during light activity that did not previously cause it
- Dizziness, fainting, or feeling like you might faint
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat noticed at rest
- Unexplained weight loss or gain
- Persistent pale skin, lips, or inner eyelids

## Menstrual-related red flags
- Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several consecutive hours
- Periods lasting longer than 7 days
- Passing large blood clots (larger than a coin) repeatedly
- Severe pain that prevents normal daily activity
- Periods that suddenly become much heavier or more irregular than usual

## Bone and calcium-related red flags
- A fracture from a minor fall or bump
- Persistent, unexplained bone or joint pain
- Noticeable loss of height over time
- A hunched posture developing gradually

## B12 and nerve-related red flags
- Tingling or numbness in hands or feet
- Difficulty with balance or coordination
- Memory difficulties or confusion that is new or worsening
- Sore or unusually smooth tongue

## Vitamin D and general red flags
- Muscle weakness, especially in the legs, making it hard to climb stairs or rise from sitting
- Bone pain that is dull and persistent, especially in the lower back, hips, or legs

## What to do
If any of these symptoms are present, the appropriate response is to consult a doctor rather than relying on dietary changes alone. These symptoms can indicate conditions that require testing (such as blood tests, bone density scans, or further evaluation) and, in some cases, treatment beyond what diet can address. Early evaluation generally leads to better outcomes than waiting to see if symptoms improve on their own.

## A note on this document's role
This chatbot can offer general, food-based information, but it is not a substitute for medical evaluation. When a user describes symptoms matching the patterns above, the appropriate response is to clearly recommend seeing a doctor, not to attempt to diagnose or manage the symptom through dietary suggestions alone.
