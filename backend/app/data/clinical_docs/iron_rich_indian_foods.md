# Top Iron-Rich Indian Foods

## Why this list matters
Knowing which everyday Indian foods are genuinely good iron sources helps translate general dietary advice into specific, actionable choices at mealtime. The list below focuses on foods that are both iron-rich and commonly available across most Indian households.

## Approximate iron content per 100g (cooked/edible portion, unless noted)

1. **Til (sesame seeds)** — ~14 mg (raw; used in small quantities as chutney or laddoo)
2. **Soybean** — ~10 mg
3. **Rajma (kidney beans, dried/cooked)** — ~5–8 mg
4. **Chana (chickpeas, cooked)** — ~5 mg
5. **Moong dal (cooked)** — ~3–4 mg
6. **Masoor dal (cooked)** — ~3–4 mg
7. **Palak (spinach, cooked)** — ~2.5–4 mg
8. **Ragi (finger millet)** — ~3.5 mg
9. **Bajra (pearl millet)** — ~8 mg (raw grain)
10. **Jaggery (gur)** — ~2–11 mg (varies significantly by source and processing)
11. **Dates (khajur)** — ~1–2 mg
12. **Poultry (chicken, cooked)** — ~1–1.5 mg (haem iron, well absorbed)
13. **Eggs** — ~1.2–1.8 mg per 100g
14. **Fish (varies by type)** — ~1–2 mg (haem iron, well absorbed)
15. **Amaranth leaves (chaulai)** — ~2–3 mg
16. **Methi (fenugreek leaves)** — ~1.9 mg
17. **Drumstick leaves (moringa)** — ~4–7 mg
18. **Groundnuts (peanuts)** — ~2–2.5 mg
19. **Watermelon seeds** — ~7–8 mg (eaten in small quantities)
20. **Pumpkin seeds** — ~3.5–8 mg

## Notes on absorption
Plant sources on this list (non-haem iron) are absorbed less efficiently than animal sources (haem iron, from poultry, fish, and meat). Pairing plant sources with a vitamin C food, and avoiding tea/coffee around meals, meaningfully improves how much iron the body actually absorbs from this list.

## Practical use
This list is most useful for building daily meals: for example, a lunch combining rajma or chana with a vitamin C garnish (lemon, tomato), or a snack of jaggery and til laddoo, adds meaningfully to daily iron intake using ordinary household foods.

## Important note
These figures are approximate and vary by preparation method, variety, and source. For precise nutritional tracking, values should be sourced from a standard reference database. This document is a general guide, not a substitute for personalized dietary advice from a doctor or registered dietitian.
