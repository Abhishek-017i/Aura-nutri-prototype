# Nutrition During the Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)

## What changes during this phase
During menstruation, the body loses blood, and with it, iron. On average, menstrual blood loss removes a meaningful amount of iron from the body over the course of the cycle, concentrated mostly in the first few days. This is why iron needs are highest during this phase compared to the rest of the cycle. Some people also experience lower energy, mild cramping, and changes in appetite during these days.

## Why iron needs rise
Iron is lost through menstrual bleeding, so regularly consuming iron-rich foods is particularly important for people who menstruate, especially when bleeding is heavy.
Hemoglobin lost through menstrual blood needs to be replaced by the body making new red blood cells, which requires iron as a raw material. If dietary iron intake does not rise to match this increased need, iron stores can gradually deplete over multiple cycles, increasing the risk of iron deficiency anemia over time. This is why a phase-aware approach to nutrition, rather than a flat daily target, better reflects what the body actually needs.

## Practical food guidance for these days
- Prioritize iron-rich foods: dal, rajma, chickpeas, leafy greens (palak, methi), jaggery, and iron-fortified foods
- Pair these with a vitamin C source (lemon, amla, citrus, tomato) to improve absorption
- Avoid tea or coffee within 1–2 hours of iron-rich meals, since tannins reduce iron absorption
- Stay hydrated, as this can help with cramping and general energy levels
- Small, more frequent meals can help if appetite is reduced

## Other supportive nutrients
- **Magnesium** (found in nuts, seeds, whole grains) is sometimes associated with reduced cramping, though individual response varies
- **Vitamin B12** supports energy metabolism during a period when the body is working harder to replace lost blood cells

## What this phase is not
This is not a time for restrictive eating or skipping meals. The body has a genuinely higher nutritional requirement during menstruation, not a lower one, so this phase calls for intentional, iron-focused eating rather than less food overall.

## When to seek medical advice
Very heavy bleeding (soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours), periods lasting longer than 7 days, or severe fatigue and breathlessness during this phase are signs that go beyond what diet alone can address. These symptoms warrant a conversation with a doctor, since they may indicate heavy menstrual bleeding that needs medical evaluation rather than only dietary adjustment.
