# Iron Deficiency Anemia in India

## What it is
Iron deficiency is an important cause of anemia, and menstruating women are at increased risk of iron deficiency because of ongoing blood loss. In India, anemia has multiple contributing causes, including nutritional deficiencies, blood loss, infections, inflammation and inherited blood disorders.

## Types of anemia
- Iron-deficiency anemia
- Vitamin B12 / folate-deficiency anemia
- Anemia due to blood loss
- Anemia associated with chronic disease/inflammation
- Inherited/genetic anemia
  - Thalassemia
  - Sickle cell disorders
  - Other inherited red-cell disorders

## Common causes
- Low intake of iron-rich foods, especially in vegetarian diets
- Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- Poor absorption due to diet composition (see inhibitors below)
- Increased need during growth, pregnancy, or after blood loss
- Underlying conditions affecting absorption (in some cases, a doctor should evaluate this)

## Two types of dietary iron
1. **Haem iron** — found in meat, poultry, and fish. Absorbed efficiently by the body, generally 15–35% of intake.
2. **Non-haem iron** — found in plant foods like lentils, spinach, and fortified cereals. Absorbed less efficiently, generally 2–20%, and strongly affected by what else is eaten in the same meal.

Since a large share of Indian diets are vegetarian, non-haem iron is the primary source for most people, which makes absorption enhancers especially important.

## Absorption enhancers
- **Vitamin C** is the most effective enhancer. Adding a source of vitamin C (lemon, amla, tomato, citrus fruit, guava) to an iron-rich meal can significantly increase how much non-haem iron the body absorbs.
- Cooking in cast-iron cookware can modestly increase the iron content of food.

## Absorption inhibitors
- **Tea and coffee**, especially when consumed with or right after meals, contain tannins that bind iron and reduce absorption. It helps to leave a gap of 1–2 hours between meals and tea/coffee.
- **Calcium** (dairy) in large amounts at the same meal can compete with iron absorption.
- **Phytates** in whole grains and legumes can also reduce non-haem iron absorption, though soaking, fermenting, or sprouting can reduce this effect.

## Practical guidance
A simple, everyday approach is to pair an iron-rich food (dal, leafy greens, jaggery, fortified cereal) with a vitamin C source at the same meal, and to avoid tea or coffee immediately before or after eating. This single habit change can meaningfully improve how much iron the body actually uses, without needing any supplements.

## Important note
This document describes food-based approaches only. It does not recommend or describe supplement dosing. Anyone experiencing symptoms of anemia (persistent fatigue, breathlessness, pale skin, dizziness) should consult a doctor for proper diagnosis and treatment, since anemia can have multiple underlying causes that diet alone cannot address.
